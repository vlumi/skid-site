---
title: "How to play"
description: "How Skid Jam plays — couch racing, drifting, and the two control schemes."
---

Skid Jam is a **top-down arcade racer** built for a phone flat on the table
and up to four thumbs around it. The whole game is one screen: the track sits
in the middle, and each player's controls live in a band on their side.

## Get set, then go

Set the players, tracks, and options, then tap **Start**. The race opens
**paused with a Play button** in the middle — everyone gets a thumb in
place first. **Tap the map** to start the countdown. During a race, tapping
the map again opens the **pause menu**.

## The two schemes

Each player picks their own control scheme in setup:

- **Casual — aim to drive.** Press and hold in your band; the direction from
  where you pressed to where your thumb is becomes the way you want to go. At
  speed, the car's body **flips into a drift** toward your aim and its
  momentum carries it round the corner. You don't manage a throttle — you
  point, and you slide.
- **Pro — direct control.** A floating pad: push forward for throttle, back
  to brake or reverse, sideways to steer. Hold a turn at speed and it drifts
  too, so you keep the arcade feel with hands-on control.

The trick with both is the same: a drift **redirects** your speed instead of
scrubbing it. Stay a little ahead of the corner and you can hold one long
slide all the way through.

## Race, or chase the clock

- **Race** — 2–4 players (or against AI), first to the flag over three laps.
  Cars can bump (**Contact**) or pass through each other (**Ghost**).
- **Time trial** — one car, chasing your own best lap. A translucent
  **ghost** of your record run drives alongside so you can see exactly where
  you're gaining or losing.

## Seating

Playing across a table? Choose **Face-to-face** (or pick the open corner for
three players) and each player's controls — and their status chip — rotate
to face them, so the game reads right from every seat.
